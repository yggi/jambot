Jambot on a phone: transport, drawer and stage

The direction chosen on #327: A and D mixed, with E for a phone on its side. The transport sits on top, and the conducting hangs from it as a drawer: Sound, Session, Style, Time, Key and Muse. A drawer sheet drops over the stage and never resizes it. At the bottom you switch the stage between four views: Song, Melody, Rhythm and Mix.

The lobby and the extras use the same frame: what plays sits on top (the metronome, the timing test, the tuner's note), the settings hang from it, and the views switch at the bottom.

The frames are live on one clock at 108 BPM. Tap play, stop (twice for a hard stop), loop, a drawer rider, a stage view, the keys and the pads.

The band's four views

  • Song is the plan and the chords read from where you play: the chord now and next, this section's bars with your motif ringed, the sections ahead, the scale the lead walks. Shapes adds guitar shapes for now and next.
  • Melody puts the roll's columns over the keys they sound. The band's foreseen notes fall toward the line; what was played passes below it, the band's in amber and yours in teal. Scale locks the keys to the scale.
  • Rhythm is the bar's pattern over the pads. Watch shows it, Edit lets you tap steps into your own pattern, Rec writes what you play on the pads into it, and Mine plays your pattern on this device. The Muse hears your pattern as it hears a motif played, so the band may take it up.
  • Mix is today's band card, unchanged: a pill per performer, its speaker showing its faders in the lane's place.

The lobby, in the same frame

  • The header is the transport of what plays on the screen: the metronome, the timing test's run, the tuner's listening (the cells become cents, the middle one in tune).
  • Hanging from it: Sound, You (the name), Settings (theme, lobby server), About.
  • The views at the bottom: Play (solo, join, host), Tune, Meter, Timing. The tuner's other tools (send a note, correct, tone) are a row of its own.

Decided, and still open

  • Loop is a button and nothing more for now.
  • Scale lock is a toggle. Chord shapes are a bonus behind a toggle.
  • Your Rhythm pattern goes to the Muse, as a motif played does.
  • The extras stay in the lobby: a session doesn't reach them.
