# The engine

Facts about `packages/engine`: its levels, the band, the Muse, the style and the time maps.
The pointers to what holds each are in the sentences.

- An engine level is a `Machine` (`packages/engine/src/machine.ts`).
  A new one gets a test that `lawBreaks(machine, script)` is empty, and the events it writes a test that `eventProblems` finds none.
- A random choice in the engine goes through `chance(draw, p)` or `pick(draw, weights)`, never a bare `draw < p`: they quantise to 2^-16, so a last-bit difference from `Math.pow` or `Math.sin` on another engine does not change the music.
  A value from them that is rounded is quantised first, `Math.round(quantize(x))`: sin(π/6) times an odd number lies one ulp from a half (`lead.ts`).
- The style (`packages/engine/src/style.ts`) is a track of glides, one per axis, and `styleAt(track, position)` reads it at any step of the song.
  Positions there are steps from the song's start, 16 to a bar.
  A track holds only the latest glide per axis, and before that glide's start it reads the glide's `from`.
  So to see what was played at a step, read the track as it stood when that step was scheduled, not the final track.
  Its axes are `sync`, `harm`, `artic`, `energy`, `entropy` and `bpm`: swing is not one (#360, the owner's ruling on #309).
- The radio (`packages/engine/src/radio.ts`, #313) is the style left alone: `walkRadio(style, seed, section)` gives the next section's targets, a free walk with no pull, every axis a uniform step in its own space, reflected at its ends.
  Entropy scales every step (0 holds the style) and holds itself; energy leans the tempo, `tempoLean` a section up at 1, as much down at 0; the tempo's step and lean together are at most 0.01 in log tempo, about 1 %, a section (the owner's rulings on #313, 2026-10-16).
  It is a machine at the section rate (`radio`), whose carried state is the style it last reached; its draws are `STREAMS.radio` by section and axis, and the session carries it, not the band card (`docs/memory/session.md`: the player's `station`, which glides the track to each style with `glideTo`).
  In C9's terms it is the style's fill with nobody in it, no read, memory or lean, so it never comes home (Q11); E30 measures its time constant, 0.3 to 0.8 hours at entropy 1 and 3.1 to 8.3 at 0.3, against the key loop's 6.67 bars, and at entropy 0.3 the station's nearest preset first changes after a median of 43 to 76 minutes, its tempo moving 4 to 8 % an hour.
  It walks the axes there are and leaves the meter's swing where it was tuned in.
- The engine's levels are machines at their own rates: the section plan (form, key and theme) per section, the band per bar, the transport per tick.
  The players' key wish is a standing target, not an event: `nextKey` applied twice gives what it gave once, so the band can read it at every section boundary.
  Where the players wish for no tonic and the mode playing, the band moves the key itself (`bandWish`, `plan.ts`, #169): `harmonicReach` (6 × `harm`, rounded) gives how many fifths, the entropy how readily (`KEY_JUMP` × entropy a section), and the harmonic history where to.
  The history (`history.ts`) folds each decided bar's chords, not its notes, so `lookAhead` and a passed bar fold exactly what then plays; the band carries it (`BandCarried.history`) and hands it to the planner at a section's boundary.
  The history is a fading memory (`fading.ts`, C9), and both of its leans are there: its centre, where the band has been, and the band's draw, by weight to the fourth power.
  Nothing hardcodes a return, and nothing makes one: the draw goes back to the key it left in a fifth to a quarter of jumps, and left alone the key ends anywhere round the circle within about 32 sections (E19, Q11).
  The wish also names a melodic scale for the lead (`KeyWish.melodic`, #209), which the plan takes at a section's boundary as it takes the key: the wish's where `licenceOf(style).melodic` reaches it, else `melodicDefault(style)` (`Plan.melodic`).
  It stands once met, as the mode does; the harmony, the themes, the numerals and the Muse keep the key's seven degrees, and only the lead's own phrases walk `melodicHelix(key, scale)`, through `phraseParams`' `scale`.
  A tonic wished for lets go once the band plans it (`released`, applied by the player), so the drift resumes from there.
  The web player only reads the audio clock and plays what the transport schedules, so everything that decides what and when is tested without a browser.
- The band decides each bar when it composes the bar before: the section's plan at a section's first bar, and the bar's chords.
  So a part can lead into the next bar's chords (`BarEnv.next`), and they are what plays.
  A bar the clock has passed is passed (`passBar`), not composed.
- A song starts and ends in the engine (`ending.ts`, `transport.ts`).
  The count-in is one bar of the song's beats before its first step, at the tempo the song opens at (`countIn`, `countInSeconds`): the player lays the clicks and starts the transport's clock a bar later, and a transport whose source is asked bars ahead composes bar 0 at once, so a host can send it during the count-in.
  A song ends on request (`finish` in the transport's input): the band composes `finaleBar` in place of the next bar it would, the transport schedules through it and no further, and `Finale.ended` says when it ended on the clock; a client is told the last bar (`ends`) and does the same.
  The final bar is written as any bar is (`finale`): the dominant seventh for two beats, then everything lands on the tonic on the third, the pad and bass held on for `RING_STEPS` past the bar, so the ending is a cadence, not a draw.
  It is decided as any bar is, so the section, key and history move on from it and a forecast from the state it leaves still runs.
- `lookAhead(carried, envAt, count)` forecasts the bars ahead, their plans and chords, by running the band's own decisions forward.
  From the transport, `forecast(env, carried, count)` gives the bars from the head's bar on, those composed first, each with the energy it plays at; with `carried` null it is the song from bar 0.
  The track holds glides ahead, so the forecast is what plays unless a control moves.
- The parts are machines at bar rate over one `BarEnv`, and `BandBar` is that environment plus the events and the bar's `sound`.
  `BarEnv.meter` is the meter the bar is in, whose steps it has (`stepsOf`, from `@jambot/theory`): the band gives the song's 4/4, and no part reads it yet, since each still writes 16 steps (#260, decision (k)); a test that builds a `BarEnv` by hand gives `meter: SONG_METER`.
  The drums read it (#260, slice 2): a bar's steps are `stepsOf(meter)`, the straight kick takes its weights from the accent tree (`accentsOf`: the downbeat, the half, the pulses), the snare's backbeat is `backbeatOf` (every pulse after the downbeat of two or three, the odd ones from four), and the two tables written on 4/4's 16 steps, the syncopated kick and the ghosts, are read by the rule in another meter (`regroup.ts`, `readOnMeter`: `cycle`, the default, which runs the pattern's four pulses through the bars and keeps the downbeat, or `fold`, the safe reading).
  4/4 was kept bar for bar by a gate recorded before the port (`docs/memory/testing.md`), and each part's test states its 4/4 literals; in any meter a hit lands on a step the bar has (`writtenProblems(hit, steps)`).
  The fold loses a hit where two land on one step and the stronger stays; in a bar of two pulses the cycle only reaches the pattern's odd pulses.
  Heard against each other, the owner preferred the cycle, most for the Funk drums in 5/4.
  The bass and the comp are on the meter too (#260, slice 3): their patterns are `patternsOn` (the root, the eighths and the walk from the grades and the pulses, the pump on the eighth after each pulse, the funk line and the syncopated stabs read by the same rule as the drums' tables) and a theme's chord slots, 0 and the bar's half, are read on the meter (`chordsOn`, `halfOf`).
  The lead, the dynamics, the style reading, the Muse, the phrase reader and the ending are on the meter too (#260, slice 4).
  The lead's groove is `straightGroove(meter)` from the accent tree and its syncopated groove is read by the cycle; a motif of any cycle is held in every meter and read by `motifInBar`: a window of the bar's steps over the motif repeated from its start, advancing by the bar's steps each bar, so 4/4 with a 16-step motif is unchanged.
  A theme's slot step is scaled by `slotStepOn`, which keeps its parity so an odd slot stays on a sixteenth.
  The dynamics' accents, the style and voice readers (`readStyle`, `readVoice`, given a meter) and the ending (`finale` lands at `slotStepOn(meter, LANDING)`, the count-in clicks once per pulse) follow `BarEnv.meter`.
  The Muse and the phrase readers take a `SongClock` (default `SONG_CLOCK`) for where bars fall, and a judged motif's cycle is the clock's `barSteps`; `BAR_LENGTHS` admits only the steps of a named meter's bar (10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24).
  A section has a meter (#261): the plan holds it (`Plan.meter`) beside the key, taken at the section boundary from the meter asked for (`BandEnv.meter`, `TransportEnv.meter`), and a bar's `BarEnv.meter` is its plan's.
  Where none is asked for and the environment carries a repository (`repository`), the composer asks it (#375, `sectionMeter`, `meter-query.ts`): the meter fragment nearest the style the band reads on `sync`, `harm`, `artic` and `energy`, held unless another is nearer by more than `METER_HOLD` (0.02), and none past `MISS_DISTANCE` (0.3), where the section keeps the meter before; the plan then carries the fragment's level, its share (`Plan.share`, which the bar's swing and the transport's beats take in place of the swing track's) and its id (`Plan.meterFragment`); with neither, 4/4.
  The repository is cells' (`repository.ts`, in the engine's barrel: `METER_REPOSITORY`, each of cells' meter fragments read by cells' compiler into a named meter and a `MeterSwing`, eleven of its thirteen, ranked by cells' `query`); node imports cells from `node_modules` through its JavaScript build (cells decision 045), so the lobby loads it at start too.
  Each preset's point finds its own meter at distance 0, so a preset held plays every event it did (15,061 over 8 presets, 2 seeds and 32 bars, to 1e-9 s, `repository.test.ts`).
  The band learns where its environment carries a learned layer (#398, cells decision 048, `layer.ts`): the song starts from it (`BandEnv.layer`, `TransportEnv.layer`), the composer asks the repository with it, and each meter it finds and keeps is a use at the section's point (`MeterRepository.used`, cells' `useFragments`), which the plans carry to the next section (`PlanCarried.layer`).
  So the band's events are made again from the seed, the style and the layer it started from, a forecast learns as the band will, and with no layer, or an empty one, it plays what it played before (`layer.test.ts`: the presets held and walked by the radio, 2 seeds, 32 bars, hashed against `main`).
  A layer weighs at most `LAYER_CAP` (16,000 bytes of JSON), the oldest points dropped first (`cappedLayer`); a filed meter is left out of the query until filing makes one, and a filed groove is a rhythm (below).
  With the section's meter found, and where the environment carries cells' rhythms (`rhythms`, `RHYTHM_REPOSITORY` in `rhythms.ts`), the composer asks for a rhythm that fits it (#397, `sectionRhythm`, `rhythm-query.ts`): the nearest drum bar at the style's point, with the meter fragment as cells' workbench so only rhythms that fit it rank, held by `RHYTHM_HOLD` (0.02) and none past `RHYTHM_MISS` (0.3) or where none fits, and beside it a line of its own that layers, a clave, within `LAYER_DISTANCE` (0.1); the plan carries both ids (`Plan.rhythmFragment`, `Plan.claveFragment`).
  A fragment that names none of the drummer's roles (`isOwnLine`: a clave) only layers on a drum bar and is never a section's rhythm by itself, so where no drum bar lies near, the drummer invents, as on any other miss (#428, the owner's ruling of 2026-10-20).
  It asks with the learned layer the plans carry, as the meter's query does, and the rhythm and clave it keeps are learned as used, each at the section's point and the two together (`RhythmRepository.used`).
  The drummer plays from them as the owner ruled on E36 (`groove.ts`): it decides each role's hits as before, and the fragment, read with the meter straight, says where they fall, its loudest first, the drummer's own on other steps making up a count the fragment can't; its fills are its own, and the clave is a role of its own, the weakest, on the claves, General MIDI's 75 (`CLAVES_NOTE`, cells v0.7.0), so it sounds beside a rim on a step they share (a fragment's hit on the rim's note loses to the rim).
  A preset held keeps the drummer's hits per role by section type within 0.2 and its fills exactly, and every other part's every note (`rhythms.test.ts`); on a miss, as in a meter no rhythm fits, the drummer invents as it did.
  The Muse files what it invents (#401, the owner's ruling of 2026-10-18), and what a player plays on drums, by one path (`muse-groove.ts`): the band carries her ear (`BandCarried.ear`), each bar the drummer plays on a miss and each player's drum bar she heard (`BandEnv.drumsHeard`, from the transport's `drums` input, a session client's arriving over the wire, #410, read onto the bar's steps by her nearest reading, `heardDrums`), and where the next section is planned she files each source's groove (`museFiled`, `plan.ts`).
  A groove is the section's recurring bar (`distilledGroove`: the pattern its bars play most, the first that plays it, ties to the earliest, 3 hits at least), stated as E31's text open on its meter (`grooveText`, whose E31 bars are cells' seed drum bars character for character, its voices holes as theirs are), filed by cells' `fileFragment` at the point that section was asked at, facet `rhythm`, `source` `drummer` or the player's name, `created` its first bar on the song's clock, `tags` `groove` and the session; a text held already adds to that fragment (identity).
  It reads back hit for hit (`readRhythm`), so filing keeps its reading and its fit to its bench and the next section compiles nothing; a filed groove is ranked as a rhythm only within the miss distance of a point, so a fit is tested only for one that could be taken.
  The composer's short-term memory (`short-term.ts`, a fading memory: half a section, in mind for the next two) holds the fragments kept and filed; every query of a section names them as chosen, and the rhythm and clave kept, and a groove filed, count as used together with the section's meter fragment, so the meter and the groove pull each other (cells decision 046).
  The composer tires of a rhythm it plays (#411, #414, `novelty.ts`, cells decision 043's novelty ladder), a seed drum bar or a groove the layer filed: each section a rhythm's family plays adds the entropy to its boredom, and each it doesn't takes it away; at 3 (`BORED`: 3 sections at entropy 1, 10 at 0.3, never at 0) the composer varies it once by an operator on a line other than the kick, the backbeat and the fill (rotate, drop or add a hit: cells' vary on the rhythm's text, yggi/cells decision 052, asked of the repository, `RhythmRepository.vary`, with the fill kept, `GROOVE_KEEP`; a rhythm of two bars isn't varied), files the variation (`source` `composer`, `varies` the rhythm, `by` the operator, line and step, as cells names it: `drop &hat 6`) and plays it, and at 6 it rejects the family, leaving it out of what the repository returns until its boredom has faded, so the next nearest plays or, where none lies near, the drummer invents again.
  The clave climbs with the drum bar it was first chosen to layer on (`ClaveOwners`, #430): never varied, and rejected and returned with that drum bar's lineage alone; layering on another rhythm meanwhile doesn't make it that one's.
  It climbs only where there is a layer to file in, so a band given none plays a preset's drum bar every section, as before.
  Every query the composer makes (the meter, the rhythm, the clave) asks with the pull of use together its place on the ladder sets (#389, `pullOf`, cells' `pull`, decision 046): until the homeostat, entropy sets it, from `PULL_AT_REPEAT` (0.2, twice cells' default) at 0 down to none at 1, so a known group holds at entropy 0 and the rhythm follows the style alone at 1.
  Over E34's walks for 3 hours (E38, E39) the layer files 195 grooves (46 the drummer's, 149 variations), the misses fall from 2,146 sections to 49, a meter no seed rhythm fits (7/8 alone since cells v0.12.0) plays its most used groove in 54 % of its sections (99.3 % before the ladder), lock-in on rhythms moves −13.4 points (within 5 with twice its error), and the layer weighs at most 14,380 bytes; with no layer nothing is heard or filed.
  A preset held at entropy 0.3 for an hour (E40) plays its run's most used rhythm in 47 % of its sections (all of them before), the drummer's hits a role by section type within 0.05 of before, and 69 % to 95 % of its hits where they were.
  The loop is one, keyed by facet (#423, `facet.ts`): the query (`nearestFigure`), the ladder (`climbedFigure`, its variations drawn from the member's stream by the fragment's place among the layer's of its facet), the Muse's filing (`filedHeard`) and the learning (`figureLearned`) serve the drums (facet `rhythm`, the clave their own step beside it) and the bass (facet `bass`), each member a `FacetRepository` that says only how it reads, states and varies its fragments.
  The bass asks after the rhythm, on the same bench (`sectionLine`, `composedLine`): cells' five seed lines (`BASS_REPOSITORY`, `bass-lines.ts`), each read by cells into notes over the chord in force (`LineNote`: step, chord 0 or the next, index into its stack, accidental, semitones; `readLine`, which agrees with cells' MIDI out over a harmony in every named meter and for every one of cells' 24 pitched variations of the seeds), a walk only under a meter that swings its 8ths (the owner's ruling on yggi/cells#209); each preset finds its pattern's line at distance 0 (root: Synth-pop, Deep house, Ambient; pump: Techno; funk: Lo-fi hip hop, Funk, Bossa nova; walk: Swing jazz).
  The section's line is carried in the plans (`PlanCarried.bassFragment`), not on the plan, which travels to clients as protocol 14 has it; a client plays the bass's notes it is sent.
  The bass plays from it as the drummer from a rhythm (`playBass`): as many notes as alone, the line's steps and tones first (its pitch class over the bar's chords, `lineTone`, the octave its own), then its own; a breakdown holds the roots, as always.
  On a miss it plays its own, and the Muse files it (`BASSIST`, tagged `bass line`) with each player's bass, a source whose every note of a bar lies at or below 57, from the notes heard or a client's part `bass` (`heardBass`, read over the bar's chords in the transport); the bass's own bar is heard once the bar after is decided, so a section's last bar isn't (`Ear.unheard`).
  A line filed is stated by `lineText` (indices on the coarsest grid, `&bassNext` and `&bassRoot` splices, its voice and card `BASS_VOICE`, the one place they are written) and reads back as filed; the composer's variation is one of cells' pitched operators (transpose, invert, reverse) around the downbeat's root.
  Held at entropy 0 (E41), the drums, the comp and the lead play every event they did, and the bass as many notes a bar, 58 % (Lo-fi hip hop) to 100 % (Swing jazz) of them on the steps they were, 29 % (Swing jazz) to 88 % (Ambient) the same note there.
  Since cells v0.10.0 a seed rhythm's, bass line's and the bell's voices are holes an instrument fills (yggi/cells#260): where the band reads or varies one, it brings the line that defines each voice the text strikes and doesn't define, from cells' instrument fragments, the General MIDI kit (`gm_kit`) first, then `bass` (`withInstruments`, `instrumentsFor`, `instruments.ts`); what is read is cells' MIDI out, which the instrument's sound doesn't move, so every query and read is as at v0.9.0 (`repository.test.ts`'s hashes).
  A groove and a bass line the Muse files are stated with their voices as holes too (`grooveText` a mix of its lines, `&groove`, and `lineText` the line, `&bassline`, neither with a MIDI card since cells v0.11.0; `BASS_VOICE` the bass's card alone, which a reading adds), so one that plays a seed's notes is the seed's text; one filed before keeps its own voices. The band's own section text plays each part through cells' seed performers (#514), compiled through cells' seed library (`compileSection`, `resolveSeed`): the drums through the drummer, which takes the 13 voices of the General MIDI kit, `gm_kit`, it strikes (cells v0.12.0 retired the core kit into it, yggi/cells#338), and the bass, comp and lead each on its performer's instrument, the bass moved an octave down from `bass`'s `C2` to keep its notes at `C1`.
  The bass's and the lead's repositories are one (`line-repository.ts`, `lineRepositoryOf`), each member's kind saying only its facet, its seeds, how a text reads and is stated, and which of cells' operators vary it; a member's `used` learns no use of a fragment the layer's cap dropped meanwhile (`stillHeld`), which a section may still play.
  The lead asks after the bass, on the same bench (#424, `sectionLead`, `composedLead`): lines of degrees of the key from the first note (`over=&key`, `LeadNote`: step, degree, accidental), a bar each, stated by `leadText` and read back by `readLead` (`lead-lines.ts`, `LEAD_REPOSITORY`); a line shorter than the bar is read as the bar, a rest after it, as a piece pads a motif into a longer meter (`paddedLead`).
  Its seeds are cells' motifs (#520, `MOTIF_LEADS`, `motifLead`, in `SEED_FRAGMENTS`): each one's head renamed `&lead`, the lead's voice beside it, stated from its first note by cells' transpose, as the lead plays it, at the point cells associates it with; so each preset plays the motif written for it (Electro swing `gypsy`, Spooky the Dies irae in its 5/4), and none fills 3/4, 6/8 or 7/8, where the lead plays its own unless the layer filed one.
  The section's line is carried as the bass's is (`PlanCarried.leadFragment`), and the lead plays it as its phrase's shape (`playLead`, `leadMotif`): the line, a variation, the line, a variation, realized as a player's motif, in the key's degrees and accidentals, never a theme's melody; an offer still wins.
  The Muse files each player's motif she judged (her own players' and a client's judgements, of a source not heard as a bass in that bar, `heardLead`, `muse-lead.ts`), and the lead's own (`SOLOIST`, `inventedBar`: the shape it draws for the bar's half, read alone so a forecast hears it) only in a section a player answered, where she heard a motif (#424's choice); tagged `lead line`.
  The composer varies a lead line by lift (`transpose &lead 2`), turn over (`invert &lead`), a bend of its last note (`bend &lead n`) and its drop (`trim &lead step`), cells' operators (`LEAD_OPERATORS`; bend and trim since cells v0.10.1); a line with an accidental isn't varied by cells (yggi/cells#268).
  On E37's walks with a player answering one section in four (E42), the most used lead line plays 34 % of the sections that play one (82 % with nothing learned, where each preset's walk stays near its motif), and over three hours with the drums, the bass and the lead filing the layer reaches its cap, which drops its oldest in 23 of 5,389 sections.
  The harmony is the loop's fourth member (#425, `harmony-query.ts`, `progressions.ts`, `HARMONY_REPOSITORY`): a progression is eight bars of triads over the key (`&harm over=&key step=1m cycle=8`), cells' five seeds the vamps of `themes.ts` (`VAMPS`), read into a theme's slots (`HarmonySlot`, `readProgression`) and stated back (`progressionText`, a seed's slots as the seed's own text).
  The composer asks for a theme where it builds one (`sectionTheme`): A asked with B chosen and B with A, so a pair used together pulls, a build with none; a vamp fits only under `VAMP_HARM` (0.33), a progression anywhere; of the fits within the miss distance, those within `OFFER` (1.25, past cells' `USE_CAP`) of the nearest are drawn by the theme's first draw, in `VAMPS`' order, so each preset at entropy 0 plays `main`'s themes slot for slot (`harmony-loop.test.ts`); a theme other than A moves off A's, and misses where A's is all there is; on a miss the tables write it (`buildTheme`).
  A vamp found is laid as `buildTheme` lays one at the section's harmony (`vampBars`), a progression as filed; the theme's progression is carried, not on the plan (`PlanCarried.themeFragments`), and a held theme whose progression the composer rejected is built anew; cells has no operators on a harmony yet, so none is varied.
  The Muse files only players' progressions (`muse-harmony.ts`): a chord is 3 pitch classes or more struck on one step, each half bar's first read as a degree of the bar's key; eight bars, a bar not read holding the chord before, from a first bar read and 4 of 8, filed tagged `progression` a section after its own ends (`PlanCarried.earlier`), as its last bars are heard only then.
  A progression is degrees, so it transposes with the key, and a key cycle (#233) moving the key under it changes nothing it says.
  The transport lays the bars on the song's clock as far as they are composed: it carries where the meter changes (`TransportCarried.meters`, the first at bar 0) and reads bars and beats from `clockOf` of them (`clockOfTransport`), learning a bar's meter as it composes or passes it (`BarSource.meterOf` gives it before a bar is composed, so a stall can pass a bar of another meter by its length).
  A bar is composed when the head is within `ahead` steps of where it starts (`barStart(nextBar) <= head + ahead`), which needs only the meters of the bars before it.
  Swing is read off the clock's beats (`swingOf(swingAt, clock)`, `swingMapAt`): a step keeps the start and length of its pulse (`ScheduledStep.pulse`), which `writtenAt` undoes its swing over.
  A note that runs into a bar not yet composed ends by the meter before it, which only moves its end where the swing does.
  `lookAhead`, `forecast` and `foresight` read each bar's environment from the clock of the bars before it, so a preview across a change is what plays.
  The meter travels in the bar's plan, and the client gives it back to the bar (`follow.ts`); the protocol is 9 since the swing left the style (#360): a bar carries its swing (`BarEnv.swing`, its plan's level `Plan.swing`), and the song and the `track` message the swing track.
  The metronome clicks the pulses of the clock it is given (`clicksOf(steps, clock, subdivision)`, the eighths or sixteenths only where asked), and swings each by its own pulse; `PRESET_GLIDE` is a number of steps, not bars, so it is 8 bars of 4/4 and more in a shorter bar's meter; the web reads the transport's clock (#262).
  The drums carry nothing; the bass its last note, the comp its last voicing, the lead its last note, its phrase and its themes' melodies (`BandCarried.parts`).
  A passed bar leaves the parts where they were, and only the lead's memory reaches more than register (S2); the band's harmonic history is the plan's, not a part's.
  The Muse judges each source's bar into a `Motif` (U4, `judgeBar` in `muse.ts`): onsets with rests, where a note was released before the next, and degrees of the key altered by `b` or `#`, written as a numeral writes a root off the key.
  The `muse` machine carries what it offers (an `Offer`: the motif, its source, the bar heard) and the last bar anyone played; its output is the log.
  The offer enters the band as its environment (`BandEnv.offer`, then `BarEnv.offer`), a standing target like the key wish, and the lead plays it whole while it stands, even where it would rest.
  The transport carries the Muse's state (`listening`) and judges bar k as it composes bar k + 2 (`LISTEN_DELAY`); a test that builds a `BandEnv` or `BarEnv` by hand gives `offer: null`.
  A motif's accents are counted from the player's level in the bar, which the judgement and the offer carry beside it as the style reading, and a motif note plays at the band's level plus its accent (U4): the lead writes it on the note (`WrittenNote.accent`), and `perform` plays it on `LEAD_LEVEL`, the level clamped to MIDI's 1 to 127 before the feel (`composedLevel`, #433), so an accent past them breathes with the feel as every other note does.
- `style-reading.ts` is the critic's reading (#15): `readStyle(dough)` reads a bar's `density`, `sync` and `artic` from its sounding notes, and `readTiming(steps)` its `bpm` from the clock, by rule.
  A reading is raw and an axis with nothing to read is absent; how far each lies from the style that wrote the bar is step 2's measurement.
  Swing is the meter's, so the style reading reads none (#360); the onsets' reader still finds it, in played time (`estimateSwing`).
- `genre.ts` (#15, step 3): `genreOf(readingAxes({ ...readStyle(dough), ...readTiming(steps, onsets) }))` is a softmax over the distances from a reading to landmarks, by default the presets' points (`PRESET_LANDMARKS`).
  A reading has `energy`, `sync`, `artic`, `bpm` and `harm`, so the distance is a root mean square over those, and E24 finds it lands a bar as its own preset in 0.53 to 0.69 of bars (0.64 to 0.80 when swing was read as an axis).
- `chord-reading.ts` (#15): `readChords(dough)` reads a root and quality per half bar from the pad and stab (the bass settles the root), and `readHarm` the `harm` of them; E25 finds it right wherever it reads and `harm` rising with the axis (tau 0.77) but short of it.
- `part-reading.ts` (#15, step 4): `readVoice(voice, dough)` reads one of four voices (drums, bass, comp, lead) alone, and `partDistance` its distance from where the voice reads at a style; E26 finds the bass says most of the style and the drums least, and a drum fill barely farther from the style than the drums' usual bar.
- The Muse is two functions and a machine over their results: `judgeBar` judges one source's bar, `taken` takes one motif of all the bar's judgements, and `muse` carries the offer.
  `reading.ts` is the one reader of onsets, shared with the analyzer (#204): `readOnsets` finds the swing (or is given it, as the Muse is, since the transport has already un-swung what she hears) and rounds.
  `phrase.ts` reads a phrase as the Muse will: `phrasesOf` cuts the notes heard at a silence of a bar less a step, `readPhrase` finds the phrase's offset, puts its first onset on the beat where the odds say so and reads each release as articulation or a plausible rest (`REST_VALUES`, `REST_MARGIN`), and snaps the onsets to the note value of the phrase (`lattice.ts`: a half, quarter, eighth or sixteenth, on the bar's own places, by the least cost of place and distance, `MERGE_LIMIT`); `readHeard` does all of it for every phrase.
  `heardInBar` is what the Muse hears of a bar, and `judgeBar` judges it, and the tracks grid's player lanes (`playedIn`, #206) show it, so a lane and the Muse never disagree: a source's notes up to the end of the bar, as phrases (`Reading` 'phrases', the default; 'nearest' is the prototype's rounding to `heardStep`, kept as the baseline), so a bar is judged by its phrase so far and never by a later bar (F7).
  `keptAfter` says what to keep once a bar is judged: the later bars' notes and each source's running phrase, up to `PHRASE_MEMORY` bars; the transport and a client's ear both use it.
  A motif's judgement carries `confidence` (hundredths, 0 to 1: the mean margin of the bar's notes from the places they were read on), and `taken` takes a motif heard before first, and a confirmed offer holds across bars against a motif heard once while she remembers it (#204 H8, Q12), then the surest reading, then the most notes (E17); adding the confidence bumped `PROTOCOL` to 4, the log's `recognised` to 5, and the Muse's memory travelling with her state (`Remembered`, `asOf`, `lastPlayed`) to 6; `memoryAt` fades it to a later bar, as her own next bar would.
  `packages/engine/experiments/humanise.ts` plays a pattern as a hand would, from one sloppiness in ms, and measures any reader over 890 bars, or 3,600 in phrases, against it; E17 records how the rounding does (69% right at 20 ms, 27% at 30, mostly lost to rests nobody meant) and how the reading of phrases does (85% and 46%).
  In a session the same split runs across apps: a client's ear (`earListen`) judges its players' bars and sends the judgements; the host's transport checks each (`admissible`: the gates a motif carries, in the bar's key, and first its shape, since it arrives as JSON) and drops, and logs, one that is late, ahead or inadmissible.
  `Motif`, `Judgement` and the bars travel as they are, so a change to their shape bumps `PROTOCOL` (`protocol.test.ts` fails until it does).
  A host reads each client message by its fields (`readClientMessage`) and refuses a name already in the session, in any case; a client reads its host's by its fields too (`readHostMessage`), as the session contract has it (#457).
  The player takes what arrived before the transport reads it, so an input it can't read costs one tick, not the song.
- A chord is the theory's `Chord`, a helix plus tensions (#38), in the key's register.
  `joinChord` gives its stack, and `chordScale` takes a stack.
  A bar's chords come from its theme's slots (`barChords`); their colour draws are keyed by theme, bar and slot, so a test that wants a rate must vary the seed or the theme, not the key.
- Swing is a time map, `leanMap`, with `played` and `written` as exact inverses: a lean per level, the 8ths' and the 16ths', signed, 0 straight, 1 the triplet, 1.5 the dotted figure, negative the offbeat early (`shareOfLean` reads a lean as the share of its span the first half holds, 50 % straight).
  The band's swing is its meter's (#360, `swing.ts`): a level, `8n` or `16n`, and a share, `MeterSwing`, which leans that level alone and never early (`leansOf`).
  Its share glides as an axis does (`SwingTrack`, `swingTo`, `glideIn`), over 8 bars from a preset and one from the Metrum card; its level is asked for at once and taken where the next section starts, as the meter is (`Plan.swing`), so a change of level never cuts into a section.
  Each beat swings on its bar's level, at the share where the beat starts.
  A note that runs past its bar ends as its bar knows the song (`laidClock`): the meters up to it and its own past it, and its bar's level, since a host holds the next bar a beat ahead and a client only as it starts (#501).
  A preset's swing is the swing of the meter cells' seed finds at its point (`presetSwing`, `repository.ts`, #459), the level and share `swingParts` gave at its old axis value, so every preset plays the onsets it did (within 1e-9, `style.test.ts` and `repository.test.ts`); the straight ones are on the 16ths.
  A preset chosen glides the share to it over 8 bars (`conductSwing`); one whose point finds no meter leaves the swing as it is.
  A meter swung on `8n` brings in the jazz kit and the walking bass, as far as its 8ths swing (`eighthSwing`, the owner's ruling on #360), until drum and bass fragments come from the repository (yggi/cells#82); the lead's pickups and the performer's feel read `swingWeight`, a 16th lean counting half an 8th's, which on every preset is its old axis value.
  A section's level is decided with its plan, on the bar before it, so a test that tours the presets a section at a time asks each preset's level a bar ahead.
  `swingMap(x)` is the one path through the two levels the readers search (`estimateSwing`): straight to a 16th shuffle at 0.5, crossfading into triplet 8ths at 1.
  The metronome screen takes the two levels directly.
  The transport plays through it, and player input reads back through `written`.
- A moment on the clock reads back onto the grid through the steps the transport laid (`writtenAt`), never through the style track: the track holds only its latest glide per axis, so inverting it from the song's start put a note 18 bars early after two tempo changes (#78).
  Each `ScheduledStep` carries the swing its beat was held at for this.
  A played note is placed at the moment that was being heard as it was played, kept exact; rounding it is the Muse's (#79).
  `clockOffset` gives that moment on both its paths, so nothing else takes the latency off again: in Chromium the heard moment lags `currentTime` by about the output latency.
  It is the device's one heard clock: MIDI out, capture, the HUD, a client's ear (`Output.heard`) and a session all read it, and `bandOutput` reads the clocks for it once a tick.
