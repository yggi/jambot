- A host keeps each bar it composes as written, with where it starts (`SentBar`, `sentBars`, from the clock of the bars it composed, `clockOfTransport`), and makes each bar's conduct from it (`conductOf`); no message carries the bar itself.
  A client's ear judges by the same clock (`earListen`), and its transport's clock is built from the conducts and the scores it was sent (`scoreSource`).

# Host and client mode

Facts about `packages/session` and the app's sessions: clocks, the fault kit and the timing panel.

- Host and client mode ([brief](../briefs/host-mode.md)): the pure parts are in `packages/session`, the app's in `apps/web/src/session/` (a `Lobby`, `HostSession`, `ClientSession`) and the player's `mode`.
  A client runs `transportOver(scoreSource)` in the host's time and plays each event `skew` earlier on its own clock; the skew is the host's heard moment less its own, so output latency needs no term of its own.
  It is three pairs of clocks composed (`skewOf`): pings are stamped with the page clocks, which never jump, and each side adds its output's offset from its page clock, which follows a dropped buffer within three ticks.
  The player reads the skew every tick while following.
  In a session a note's source is a player's id and the instrument (`playerSource`), never a name; screens read it by name through `sourceName` and the players' names the host sends with the Muse.
  An output's clock pairing steps while it starts, so `clockOffset` reads no pairing for its first 0.2 s (`PAIRED_AFTER`).
  To try it, open the app in two windows of one browser, host in one and join in the other; `session.e2e.ts` measures the two.
  It holds one page's audio (`holdAudio`, a `suspend` and `resume`): 40 ms is a jump of some 30 ms, 80 of some 70; holds of 20 ms or less jump the pairing anywhere from −10 to +11 ms, as the output settles again.
  It stalls a page's main thread with a busy loop.
  `heard` reads when a note sounded through the pairing sampled as it sounded (a median of five, as a single reading can blip); `clockJumps` finds each jump of a page's clock, and `stalls` each stall of its main thread, from its 5 ms sampler coming late.
  So a check bounds every note by the jumps settling as it sounds and excuses exactly the notes a host's stall dropped, on any machine, rather than count notes out.
  A clock's discontinuity is a jump, so a step stays the grid's.
  Across machines, run `pnpm lobby` on one and give its `ws://` address as the lobby server on each; every machine loads the app from its own `localhost`, where Web MIDI works without HTTPS.
- A host tells its clients the song as its count-in begins, a bar before the song's first step (`start`'s `Song.start` is in that bar's future), so a client has that bar to pair its clocks and to receive bar 0, which the host has composed already; a client clicks the rest of the count-in and plays the song from the host's start.
  A host that is asked to stop plays through a last bar that closes on a cadence and tells its clients which bar it is (`finale`); a client plays through it too and halts when its ring has run out, as the host does, so nobody lacks a bar past the end, and the host's `stop` follows when it halts.
  A second stop, or any stop that isn't a person's (leaving a session, following a host), halts at once (`Player.halt`).
- `Sessions` (`apps/web/src/session/sessions.svelte.ts`) is the page's one place for opening, joining, ending and restoring: a session is `local` (the tabs lobby) or `global` (the lobby server at `address`, kept for next time, empty for none), and a page is in one at a time.
  It lists both lobbies with each session's players (`list`), and a lobby it can't reach is a `problem` beside the ones it could.
  The lobby server counts the host and every client socket it holds; a client whose link dropped is not counted while away, and present and away are not told apart.
  The tabs lobby counts who said they joined and left, so a tab closed without leaving is counted until the session closes.
  Tested against `memoryLobby` (`session/fixtures.ts`), without a component.
- A test of anything that reads a clock runs it on `simulatedDevice` (`packages/session/src/faults.ts`, #27): real time is the test's, and each fault is named, else off: drift, dropped buffers (the output's clock stands still for the buffer), the render quantum, latency, the pairing's jitter and blips, page stalls, and a link's legs, holds and loss.
  `device.view(() => t)` gives the `clock` and `now` the app reads, and `{ paired: false }` a platform with no pairing.
  The quantum reaches only `currentTime`, so a reader of the pairing meets it only on its fallback.
  A claim is a bound the component promises, and a test shows its checker firing.
  Measured here in headless Chromium, the pairing is within about 0.4 ms but a busy machine blips a reading by up to 4 ms, which the next takes back; a dropped buffer jumps it for good.
  Where a component doesn't keep its promise, the scene is kept as a known break, a test that finds it, until it does.
- The web tests resolve `ws` to its Node build (`apps/web/vitest.config.ts`): resolved for the browser it is a stub, and `websocket.test.ts` runs the real lobby server, in Node's environment.
  Its devices' clocks are the test's and stand still, so a ping comes back at once and the skew is exact on any machine; the clocks under delay are the pure tests'.
- `jam` (`packages/session/src/session.test.ts`) runs a host and a client over a link that drops bars, or, given a `recovery`, keeps order as TCP does (`orderedArrival` in the fault kit): a lost message is sent again after the recovery and holds up everything behind it, and the pings' answers, the bars ahead and the Muse share the stream.
  A bar is lacked exactly where the stream held it past a beat (T13).
- `?timing` in the address shows the timing panel (`TimingPanel.svelte`, #27): the transport's tick intervals on the page clock, how far ahead of the clock each event was scheduled, the steps laid and missed, the events dropped late (#462: each whose onset the clock had passed as its step was laid, or whose step it passed whole in a bar the transport held), the output pair's jumps (its dropped buffers), a client's round trips and skew, bars too late and judgements dropped.
  Export saves them as `jambot-timing-….json` (`timingReport`), the file to attach to a timing issue.
  They count from the song's start, and a client's clock from its join, and are kept after a stop until the next start.
  A spread keeps whole-millisecond bins up to a second, so a quantile is good to half a millisecond at any length of run.
  `timing.e2e.ts` holds the export to what the harness measures of the same page.
  The export also carries, under `midiLinks`, the latest MIDI link report of each output that was measured, by its key in the setup (webmidi's `LinkReport`, decision 009, as it made it; `{}` until a person measures), and the panel shows each one's two tiers (scheduled, played) and why, marked speculative: webmidi's limits are proposals until real devices' numbers replace them (#27 step 5).
  A link is a loop through an output and an input (a cable or a device that echoes) timed in parts: the loop, the page's lag, one way as an assumption (loop over two); it is not the device's latency offset, nor the sound path, which needs the audio loop (step 5's other half) and is not measured.
- The radio (#313) is the session's, not the band card's (the owner): the player's `station` (`packages/session/src/station.ts`, a seed and the sections walked) is on the air from `tuneRadio`, which the Style card's Radio switch turns, on by default, for a host or a band alone.
  At the first bar of each section after the song's first the player walks the style (`#walkRadio`): `walkRadio` from where the track is going, the players' tempo nudge taken out (`radioStyle`), and the track glides there over one section from the later of the bar's start and the head (`conductRadio`, `RADIO_GLIDE`), so what is scheduled stays as it is; the nudge is held within the new style's licence.
  The walk's state is the style track's targets, with no second copy, so a slider moved is the walk moved, and a preset or an axis set is where it walks on from; only the switch takes it off the air (the owner, #627).
  The station survives a stop and a reload (`HostSnapshot.station`, absent when off), and a client has none: the glides travel in the `track` message as the host's player made them, exact doubles through JSON, so a client follows the walk to the last bit, and one that joins mid-walk gets the track in its welcome (`session.test.ts`); the protocol is unchanged.
  The Style card names where the band is and where it heads, as the nearest presets (`nearestPreset`, `nowPlaying`), from the track alone, so a client reads it as the host does: "Custom · drifting toward Ambient".
- A client's ear passes on what its players play on drums a bar a message (`played`, a `PlayedBar`, #410), as it judges the bar, a beat after the bar ends: the notes as heard, the earliest `PLAYED_MOST` (64), about 85 bytes each as JSON.
  The host names them by the player it seated (`heardFrom`), never by what the client says, and hands them to its transport's `drums` as its own player's, so its Muse files a groove a client plays over a section under the client's name, the same layer as if the host's own player had struck the notes (`session.test.ts`).
  A bar's drums reach the Muse over a way back of up to a beat and a half (0.5 s at 180 bpm, 1.5 s at 60), as a judgement does, and a later bar is dropped unheard; a drum note for a bar the ear passed on already is dropped there.
  The message names its part (`part`, any of `PARTS`), so a pitched part's notes travel the same way: a client's ear also passes on, as the part `bass`, a bar a player played wholly in the bass's register (every note at or below 57, `BASS_RANGE`'s top, #423), and the host hands those to its transport's `bass` (`heardFrom(played, name, 'bass')`, `Player.bassElsewhere`), so its Muse files the line under the client's name.
  A client's players' motifs need no part: the judgements it sends are what the host's Muse files as lead lines (#424), under the source she knows them by (`playerSource`, `@id/instrument`).
- A host whose band plays through cells, the default (#456), sends each section's score as it takes it from its worker (`score`, a `SectionScore`, #446, #553): the cells text, its first bar and step, the section's type, the fragments it locks, each bar's energy, a digest of what each bar was built from (`foreseen`), the bars a note was cut in, and why none states the bars where none does; a welcome carries those that state a bar not yet played (`song.scores`).
  The score travels, not the events cells plays from it: it is legible, and its text 1.6 to 3.1 KB a section as JSON against 11 to 89 KB for the events (`section-texts.test.ts`), one message a section and one more for each bar composed otherwise than foreseen.
  What travels of the engine's own types (a conduct's offer, a judgement, the Muse's log and memory, a played bar) is held by `protocol.test.ts`'s `SHAPES`, and the hub's session contract holds the same shapes of a judgement, the log, the memory and a played bar.
- From protocol 30 on, every protocol is a minor of the session contract's version 2 (yggi/music-commons `contracts/session`; the owner's rulings, #460, yggi/music-commons#57): 30 is minor 0, a section's `score` and each bar's `conduct`; 31 minor 1, a player's nudge of the key and the meter, the session's log (`event`, a welcome's `events`), a judgement's `origin` and the style's `mood`; 32 minor 2, a client's `lacked` (yggi/music-commons#74); 33 minor 3, a score's section a kind of a song form (#655, yggi/music-commons#76).
  A host admits a join of any of them (`sameMajor`, `FIRST_PROTOCOL` beside `PROTOCOL`), refusing one below 30 for its version, and a client whose welcome says below 30 sends `leave` and goes; each side drops a kind it doesn't know, so a minor that adds a kind deploys without every peer in lockstep.
- Jambot speaks version 2 of the session contract alone (yggi/music-commons `contracts/session`, minor 3, protocol 33, #553), and no version before it: the owner's ruling of 2026-11-24 carries no legacy before 1.0.0.
  A host admits a join of any minor of version 2 (Cell studio's follower joins at 31) and refuses one before it, a page cached from before, for its version; a client leaves a host whose welcome says a protocol before it, saying `leave`.
  A welcome of the version before doesn't read as a host's message (`readHostMessage`), so a client that joins such a host reads none of its words and ends as one no host answered (`WELCOME_WAIT`).
  A client plays its host's scores alone, on the clock its conducts give (`followScores`, `followConduct`, `followScore` over `scoreSource`); the player has no other way to follow a host.
- The messages (#553): `protocol.ts` holds them, a host's `score` and `conduct` and a welcome with `conducts`, `scores`, `fragments` and `band`, which `readHostMessage` reads, and a client's, with minor 2's `lacked`, which `readClientMessage` reads; the lobby's are `lobby.ts`'s (`readToLobby`, `readFromLobby`), and each reader's checks are on those in `fields.ts`.
  `sameMajor`, `refusal` and `admit` take version 2's protocols alone; `contracts.test.ts` runs the contract's vectors.
- A host (#553): `HostSession` admits joins of version 2, sends each bar's `conduct` (`conductOf`: the bar composed with where it starts, and its digest, `foreseenOf`) and each section's `score` (`hostScoreOf`, from what the shadow told), and welcomes with the conducts, the scores in force (`scoresInForce`), the fragments they lock that the client lacks and the default band (`DEFAULT_BAND`).
  A score's `lock` is cells' `textLockfile` of its text but `band`, and its `filed` the locked fragments not in the seed library at the pin (`scoreLock` in `packages/score`, worked out where the text is built); each client is sent those it lacks once by hash (`fragmentsLacked`), the ledger kept by its seat's id, so a rejoin by token sends nothing again, and dropped with the seat.
  The shadow asks for a section's bars in runs that each hold one meter, swing, key and melodic scale (`split`, `sectionRuns`), so a looper over two keys leaves no bar unstated, as one text a section did (#648): `host-v2.test.ts` holds it.
- A client (#553): `ClientSession` joins at `PROTOCOL`, and leaves a host whose welcome says a protocol before version 2's first.
  A lobby hands a client a host's message it reads (`readHostMessage`), and the client reads it again, as `memoryLobby` reads none.
  The player runs its transport over `scoreSource` (`follow-v2.ts`): each bar's conduct, and what the latest score that states it states of it (`StatedBar`), which the shadow reads off the score as it is given (`givenScore`, `playScore` in the worker) and says first as `reading`.
  A score's meter, swing, tempo and key are read off cells' compile of it (`scoreFrame` in `packages/score`: the main meter's cell and clock, the `diatonic` take), and each bar's `artic` and `sync` off its `&style` (cells' `styleAt`); the bar laid is the score's, framed by the conduct's seed, entropy and offer, its notes none, so nothing but cells' events plays.
  A bar plays only where its conduct carries the digest its score foresaw; else it is lacked (`lacked`), a bar no score states plays silent (`silent`, the Session card's "No score stated"), and the latest of each are said with why (`said`, `SAYS`).
  Each such bar is told to the host once (`lacked`, minor 2: `bar`, `why`, `text`), its why the contract's by `LACKED_AS` (`follow-v2.ts`): no conduct, or a score still read, is `late`; no score, or one `unstated`, is `silent`; a digest not the score's is `digest`; a score that locks a fragment the client lacks, or that cells reads no frame in, is `lock`.
  The host counts them by the client's seat and why (`HostSession.lacked`, `countLacked`), whatever its role, and shows each player's beside its name on the Session card, its whys on hover.
  The dev readout's `window.jambot.lacked` sums them (`lackedIn`), and the session soak's bound reads it: 0 bars lacked, 0 late (`lackedBreaks`).
  The harmonic history folds what cells plays of each score's harmony (`foldHeard`).
  The fragments a host sends are kept by hash (`ClientSession.fragments`), given with each score that locks them (`scoreResolver`; one neither they nor the pin hold lacks the score's bars, `lockLacks`), and kept with the resume record (`ClientResume.fragments`), so a page that reloads and rejoins by its token holds what its seat isn't sent again.
  A client keeps the last 8 bars' scores and conducts whatever plays (`KEPT_BARS`, `keptFrom`, `Player.kept`), for a loop of its own (#626).
- A band alone or hosting sounds through its own player (#711, step 2 of `docs/briefs/conductor-and-player.md`): the conductor's transport composes, listens and runs the Muse, its events laid by no one, and hands the page's own player each bar's conduct (`conductOf`, its digest the shadow's) and what each score its band takes states (the shadow's `stated`), by direct calls at a skew of 0, which it plays over `scoreSource` as a client does.
  Each score is built and played as a client plays it (`stateSection` through `playScore`'s reading, with the fragments it locks), and handed over with what cells played, so nothing compiles twice; its bars are `reading` from its ask until its answer, and lacked if laid meanwhile.
  So a host and its clients play one path, event for event while the radio glides the style (`session-v2.test.ts`), and a bar no score states plays silent and is counted; the Muse still hears the page's own players through the conductor's transport (chords, hands, the bass's register).
- A host admits anyone who finds its session, under any free name, until it asks for a code (#461, the owner's ruling on yggi/music-commons#22: a name is enough while a client only listens, a code before clients contribute): the Session card's "Admit players by a code" draws one (`HostSession.admitByCode`, `codeOf`, `CODE_DIGITS` 6 from the browser's `crypto`), shows it, and draws another on asking; the Host card's "By code" opens a session asking for one from the start.
  From then on a join with another code is refused with `CODE_WRONG`, looked at before the name (`refusal`), and one without a code is seated to listen (`Member.role`, the welcome's `listening`, `CODE_LISTENS` shown on the client's card): it hears the band, and the host drops what it plays, judges or asks to forget, so a listener files nothing (the owner's ruling on #479).
  The host may seat any client as a player or a listener at any time (`HostSession.cast`, "Let play" and "Make listen" by each player on its Session card, #567), open or by code: it tells that client alone with `role`, and the seat's token brings it back as that role.
- A player nudges a style axis or the tempo (`nudge`, step 2 of `docs/briefs/session-roles.md`): the host weighs it a step over the players present and drops a listener's (`nudged`), each section line releases half of what is pending (`releaseNudges`), and the style leans by it, on the radio's walk or alone (`leaned`, `Player`'s `#sectionLine`); a host keeps no nudges through a reload, since they fade anyway.
  The key and the meter move only by whole steps: a section line takes one where what is pending comes to half a step, the key's tonic a fifth from where the band heads, held within reach (`Player`'s `#stepKey`), the meter a pulse more or fewer where that is a meter on offer (`meterNudged`, `#stepMeter`); nudges pending fade at every section line, those that take no step too.
  A player seated before stays seated, and one whose resume token opens its seat comes back without the code, a listener as a listener unless it brings the code (`roster.ts`, kept through a reload in `HostSnapshot`).
  Five wrong codes within a minute across the session lock it: every join with a code is refused with `CODE_LOCKED` until the oldest of them is a minute old (`WRONG_CODES`, `wrongCodesWithin`), so a million codes can't be walked.
  The code travels only in a join, never in a host's message or the lobby's list, so the lobby reads none and needs no deploy; a client that carries none joins an open session as ever and is seated to listen by one that asks for a code, Cell studio's listener among them.
  The code is kept through a reload (`HostSnapshot.code`, absent in older records: open), and a client sends the one typed at the door's Join card with every join of that session.
- The session has one log, human-readable and like a chat (`event` and a welcome's `events`, step 3 of `docs/briefs/session-roles.md`): the host words each line (`apps/web/src/session/log-lines.ts`), the players' (joined, back, left, lost, cast, nudged) in `HostSession`, the conductor's at each section line (`Player`'s `said`), the composer's for each section as it comes next (from `decided` and `composed`), the Muse's from her log (`muse`); it keeps the last 200 and a welcome carries the last 50 (`log.ts`), and a client keeps what it is told (`ClientSession.log`).
- Jambot passes the session contract's version 2, minor 3 (protocol 33): `contracts.test.ts` runs every vector of its copy, `packages/session/fixtures/contracts/session.json` (lifted from yggi/music-commons `contracts/session` at 6255cbf, 381 vectors), through `readClientMessage`, `readHostMessage`, `readToLobby`, `readFromLobby`, `admit` and `sameMajor`, and records by name, with its reason, any it doesn't meet (none, #457).
  A client checks a host message's fields as a host checks a client's (`readHostMessage`, the contract's ruling), so a message of a known kind malformed is dropped and the link kept, as a kind it doesn't know is.
  To move to a later minor, copy the hub's file again with its `lifted` line naming the new commit, and its own `lifted` as `hubLifted`.
- A lobby's list says `away: true` for a session it holds for a host whose link dropped, and leaves it out while the host is there (#496); `readFromLobby` refuses a list whose `away` is anything but a boolean, and the door marks an away session and offers no join to it, which the lobby would refuse.
- The lobby server reads no protocol version: it relays a client's and a host's messages as they are (`readToLobby` checks only that a message is an object), so a bump of `PROTOCOL` deploys the page alone, and the lobby redeploys when `apps/lobby` or `packages/session/src/lobby.ts` changes (`lobby.test.ts` relays a client's played bar and a host's score, which it never reads).
- A timing claim is tested by the work it bounds, never by the wall clock: an advance of the transport passes at most `CATCH_UP` bars (a day's stall is some 30,000), and the ticks after carry on, so no advance outlasts its lookahead on a slow machine (T1).
- What the band learned (#398) is the host's and has no age, so it is kept beside the snapshot, not in it: `layer-store.ts` holds the interface (`LayerStore`, an async `load` and `save`, so a store across the network fits it later), a store in memory, and a store over a slot of text, each layer kept as a versioned record (`layerText`, format `jambot-learned-layer`, version 2: the layer as `storedLayer` keeps it, its groove variations by reference, #553) that reads back byte for byte; a record of version 1 reads as it did.
  A text that doesn't read (not JSON, another format or version, a layer a query would refuse) loads as the empty layer with one warning; a reference that can't be rebuilt is left out and named in one warning; a client keeps none.
- A link that drops is no leaving (`docs/briefs/host-mode.md`, "When a link drops"): the roster (`roster.ts`) seats a player by the token its welcome gave, the lobby holds a session for its host's secret (`LOBBY.hostGrace`), and the host's snapshot (`snapshot.ts`, `session/storage.ts`) is what a reload needs.
  `lost`, `away` and `back` are said by a lobby adapter, never read off the wire (`readClientMessage` and `readHostMessage` refuse them).
  A client's ids are the lobby's per socket (`c0`, `c1`, …), and restart from `c0` with the lobby, so the roster never gives a new player an id an away one holds.
  The web tests' `memoryLobby` has `cut()` to drop every client's link and `refusing` to refuse joins; the real-socket ones wrap `WebSocket` to close one as a phone's system would, without a word.
  `memoryLobby` answers at once, but a client's `join` returns as soon as the join is sent, before the host's welcome: a test of what shows in between holds the host's answers (`held`, `sessions.test.ts`, #202).
  The host's kept snapshot and the client's resume have versions of their own (`snapshot.ts`): a change to a shape inside one, such as `KeyWish` in the host's, bumps that one's version alone, so a client can still rejoin across a deploy (#226).
  A field added that an older record can lack, such as the host's `meter`, keeps the version: reading takes its absence as the default.
  Explicit close sends `end` to the lobby: without it the name would be held for the grace.
- A browser holds an audio context suspended until a click, and on a reloaded page `resume` stays pending for good, so a restore must not wait for it: opening and joining ask for the output in the click's turn but wait `OUTPUT_WAIT` at most (`Player.prepareWithin`), and a song's start waits for it by itself.
  A restore that gets no answer is given up after `RESTORE_WAIT`, forgotten, with its reason in `ended`; `Sessions.drop` (the restoring screen's "Drop session") lets a person do the same at once, and a restore or join that was dropped meanwhile closes or ends itself when it arrives.
