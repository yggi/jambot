# The web app

Facts about `apps/web`: the screen, its components and what its end-to-end checks rely on.

- The screen reads one snapshot, `player.hud` (`hudOf`, in `apps/web/src/hud.ts`), rebuilt when the step heard, the bars ahead or the track change.
  Components take slices of it as props and send intents; none reads the engine.
  A style is named by the preset it matches on every axis the preset sets but energy (`presetOf`), so there is no chosen-preset state: moving another axis makes a style custom, and moving energy or entropy, or nudging the tempo, doesn't.
  `StylePanel.svelte` is the style card, folded by default: the closest named style as its main element (`styleName`: the preset, else Custom), what the band does as the sub element (`styleSub`: "Playing Synth-pop", "Gliding to Ambient", or for a custom style "Custom · swung, sparse", the up to two axes furthest from the middle, `styleWords`), and as the glyph a radar of the six axes (`Radar`), drawn from `hud.style.axes`, the axes of the style at the step heard (`styleAt`), so a glide moves it while the gauges, like the old sliders, show where each axis is going.
  Unfolded, the presets are a `Segmented` choice in two rows of four (the component the key's scale uses, given `columns`; the four corner cells are curved to the outline so a pressed one's ring follows it), and each axis is a `Gauge` with a transparent range input laid over it (the drag) and a `Nudge` of a tenth of the axis.
  The host, or a band playing alone, picks, drags and nudges; a client's drag and presets rest and its nudges are disabled, titled "The host sets the style", and its sub says so, since `Player.apply` drops a client's style intents as it does its key intents.
  What a client's nudge of an axis means, and its path to the host, is open (#217).
  `KeyPicker.svelte` is folded to a summary by default: the key heard (`hud.bar`'s, else the one the band is heading for) in common terms as its main element (`keyName`: "C major", "A minor"; ionian and aeolian are major and minor, `MODE_NAMES`), its notes ascending from the tonic as the sub element ("A B C D E F G", with the melodic scale after them where it isn't plain, "· pentatonic", `BarView.melodicName`), and as the glyph the wheel (`CircleOfFifths` `compact`: the figure, the tonic and the harmonic history's radar under them, no names) at the key the band is heading for (`hud.heading`, the last bar decided's), so a modulation shows before it is heard.
  A key and its notes are spelled in the key, as its signature has them (`noteNames.ts`, on the theory's `spelledTonic` and `spell`, as the engine's `keyName`): G♯ minor, not A♭ minor. The key card, the transport and the Song view all name a key this one way; only the wheel, where a pitch class stands alone, uses the prototype's fixed `TONIC_NAMES`.
  Unfolded it shows, stacked at every width and with no switch between them, two views of the one key that complement each other: the circle of fifths selects and nudges the tonic and shows the scale's shape on it, and under it the scale selects the shape and draws it as a mark on each note and the octave, on the 12 steps of the octave (`scaleMarks`), the gaps between the marks showing the whole and half steps (`scaleSteps`), with no other labels or captions.
  The scale's selector is a segmented button of all seven diatonic modes, brightest to darkest; the band plays four (`COMPOSER_MODES`), so the other three are disabled.
  Only major and minor keep their full names in it; the other modes are their first three letters (`MODE_SHORT_NAMES`: lyd, mix, dor, phr, loc), with the name as tooltip and accessible name, never Roman numerals, which the chords level below writes for chords.
  Changing the shape keeps the tonic, and nudging the tonic keeps the shape, the two going through the same `key` intent.
  Under the scale, a second segmented button picks the melodic scale the lead walks over the key (plain, pentatonic, blues; #209), checked at the one the band heads for (`hud.headingMelodic`), those beyond the style's reach disabled, resting on a client as the key does.
  A `key` intent names what it changes of the wish (`Partial<KeyWish>`), and the rest stands: a tonic and mode picked keep the melodic scale wished, and a scale picked keeps the key.
  `CircleOfFifths.svelte` draws it: theory's `fifthsIndex` (built on `generator(7, 12)`) lays the 12 pitch classes out by fifths, and the current scale is the arc its dots make along the rim, open and in fifths order (`figureArc`), with the tonic alone ringed; only a scale with a hole in it (harmonic minor) gets a dashed chord across the wheel.
  It is read-only wherever it is given no `mode`/`onintent`; given both, a dot is a button that wishes for its pitch class as tonic, in the mode already playing (going through the same `key` intent the mode select does).
  A key picked is wished for tonic and mode; a preset wishes for its mode only when the wish is in another, so a key picked survives a preset in its mode.
  `history`, always drawn on the same wheel, is one weight per pitch class from `player.harmonicHistory`, drawn as a filled radar under the wheel.
  The host, or a band playing alone, sets the key (the dots and the shape) and nudges it, by the Nudge up and Nudge down buttons, a fifth clockwise or counter-clockwise in the mode heard, within the licence.
  A client's key intent is dropped by `Player.apply` until the host takes nudges over the session, so a client's nudges are disabled, titled "The host sets the key": the buttons are the seam, the transport is open.
  It is the band's own harmonic history, the one its key follows (`packages/engine/src/history.ts`, #169): each bar decided folds in its chords' pitch classes by their length, after decaying the rest, so the shape reads as "what has the band's harmony been lately", not the current scale.
  While the band plays here the radar reads the engine's carried history; a client folds the host's bars the same way as they arrive, so after a stall it lacks the bars it never got until they decay away.
  A tonic picked lets go once the band has planned it (`released`), so the band moves the key on from there by itself.
  The chords card doesn't name the key: the key card's summary does.
- The band (`screens/Band.svelte`) is a screen in the shell (`Shell.svelte`, #327), which owns the viewport so the page never scrolls: the transport on top, a drawer hanging from it, and the views below, switched at the bottom.
  The drawer's riders are Sound (the page's Sound card, handed in as a snippet, since it is on both sides of the door), Session, Style, Time (the metrum), Key and Muse, and Counters (the timing counters) where the address asks for them (`?timing`); a rider drops its card as a sheet over the views, one at a time, and the rider again, the sheet's grab ("Hide Style"), the scrim or a view takes it up.
  The views are Song (the plan and the chords), Melody (a roll of the lead over the keys), Rhythm (the play card on the drums: its lane over the pads) and Mix (the band card); Song shows first.
  Every sheet and view stays mounted and those not shown are `hidden`, so a card keeps what it shows; a test or a check that reaches a control opens its section or view first, as a person would (`openCard` and `showView` in `audio.ts`, `pull` and `view` in `App.test.ts`), since a hidden element has no role.
  Under the shell a card is pinned open (`pinCards`, a context `Card` reads): its summary heads its details and a click on it folds nothing.
  The regions are each one height, a view and a sheet keep their scrollbar's room (`scrollbar-gutter: stable`) so a scrollbar coming never narrows them, and the shell's one column is `minmax(0, 1fr)`, since the transport's longest line would otherwise widen it past a 360 px screen.
  On a phone on its side (`max-height: 500px`, landscape) the views are a rail at the left and the riders sit beside the transport.
  The session, style and key cards rest their own controls where a client takes the host's.
  The transport heads the band's screen and stays in view as the page scrolls (`Transport.svelte`, `transportView.ts`, #327): Start the band / Stop, which a client's rests (titled "The host starts and stops the band"), the section and bar heard ("Groove 3/8") over the tempo, the meter and the key, and the bar as its background, a cell per pulse as wide as the pulse is in eighths (`transportCells`: 7/8 as 3+2+2), the cell heard lit and its eighths as dots (`transportHeard`).
  In the count-in it counts the beats left ("Count-in · 3", from `Player.countStep`, the step of the count-in's bar heard) and hatches the cells still to come; while the band ends its button is an outline and its second line says a second Stop stops it now.
  Its loop toggle holds its own state and is wired to nothing yet: what holding a section means is open (#327).
  It is one height, 64 px, whatever it says.
  Under it the Session card (`Session.svelte`, `sessionState.ts`) says who the band plays with and is the way out.
  Its main element is the session by name ("Garage", or "Solo"); its sub element is the state, or "Solo · counting in" and "Solo · ending" (and the like for a host and a client) while the song begins and ends ("Hosting · 2 joined", "Following · host playing", "Reconnecting · playing on what it has", "This device only"); its glyph is the players as a network (`Network`: the host in the middle, the others round it, each in its player's colour, pinging on each bar heard).
  Start counts a bar in with the click (`Player.counting`, `Metronome.countIn`: the click's own volume, whether it is on or not) before the band comes in.
  Stop asks the band to end (`Player.ending`): it plays to its next bar's end on a cadence and lets the last chord ring before it halts, and a second Stop, or a Stop in the count-in, halts at once.
  Its details are the players of a host (`You (host)` and those who joined), or the link of a client (its name, the round trip, the bars that came too late), and the way out: Close session for a host, Leave session for a client, Back to the lobby from solo.
  Closing or leaving asks first, in place (`role="alertdialog"`), and a host's question says how many are playing in the session.
  An e2e check that plays alone enters the band first (`solo` in `audio.ts`); one that hosts or joins works at the lobby (`host`, `join` in `session.e2e.ts`).
  The About card's footer signs the lobby: "all rites reversed", a pentagon, "kallisti collective, 3192 YOLD".
  A check that picks a preset or moves a slider pulls the style's section down first (`openStyle` in `audio.ts`).
- The page (`App.svelte`) is the container, and shows one of three screens (`screenOf`, `screen.ts`): the door, the band behind it, or the door saying a kept session is taken up again.
  The door (`screens/Door.svelte`) is in the same shell as the band.
  Its drawer holds what belongs to the person and the device: Sound, You (`You.svelte`: a name made up already, `Profile`, kept, with a Regenerate button beside it), Settings (`Settings.svelte`: the theme, `theme.svelte.ts`: system, dark or light, kept, set as `data-theme` on the root, and the lobby server's address), About (`About.svelte`: the logo glyph, "Jambot", what the band is, the signature), and Counters with `?timing`.
  Its views are Play (the lobby, `screens/Lobby.svelte`), Tune, Meter and Timing (the extras); Play shows first, and the page holds which shows (`doorView`), not kept, so a reload lands on Play, and a session or Solo puts it back to Play.
  Its header is what the view plays: on Play a banner of the page's name, the person's and the sessions open; on an extra the `Transport` with that extra's labels (Start, Play, Start the test or Run again, and Stop), its bar's cells for the metronome and none for the others, no cadence and no loop.
  An extra runs only while its view shows: leaving it stops the test, the metronome or the tuner, and the timing test takes the computer's keys and MIDI's taps only there.
  Every view stays mounted, so the extras' screens are drawn at the door whichever shows.
  A cold start shows the lobby, in the band's own cards: Play Solo (the session card's single dot as its glyph, "local band"), the sessions open to join, and the host card.
  The host card is its summary alone: the session name (made up by `randomSessionName`, with its own Regenerate button), a Local | Global segmented choice, each with a small glyph, and Host beside it, over a note that says where the session will be ("In this browser: its tabs and windows can join", "Through the lobby server: others can join").
  A join button shows the players in the session, and is teal for a session in this browser and amber for one through the lobby server, with a tooltip.
  The lobby server's address is in the door's Settings, and is given when the field is left.
  A reload returns to a session still kept, a host's or a client's, and lands on the lobby from anything else, solo included: solo is a choice the page holds and doesn't keep.
  A client that is still joining stays at the lobby, busy; one whose host closed, or whose link was given up, is back at it, with the reason.
  The band (`screens/Band.svelte`) is the levels; the screens take props and send intents, and lint keeps them from running the player, the device or the sessions (only their types), or from importing each other.
  What outlives the door is the container's: `Device` (MIDI settings and connection, the audio clock and output), `Instruments` (the routes of the keys, the pads and MIDI in, and what is held), `Profile` and `Sessions`.
  The keyboard and the pads are the band's, behind the door; the Sound card is on both sides of it.
  The computer's keys play only behind the door.
  Back at the lobby nothing plays on: the page stops the player, which rests the click.
  The master volume (`Device.volume`, a `Fader`) is the container's too, the Sound card's main element, so it is worked from the folded card and is above both screens: local, kept, and the square of its position is the synth's gain (`taper`).
  It scales the built-in synth only (`Output.level`, on the bus that `silence` replaces), not MIDI out; the click is on the same bus, so it is scaled and faded with the band, on top of its own volume.
  A `Fader` holds no value and is the level a mixer's tracks will share.
  The click that joins or hosts is the gesture that lets the browser play audio: `Sessions` asks for it before its first await, and a test holds it there.
  Joining mutes the master, and it rises over `FADE_IN` once the band plays; leaving, closing or going back plays on while it falls over `FADE_OUT`, then the volume is put back for what plays next.
  A new widget goes into the level it belongs to, not into a corner of another.
- A section of the band's drawer, and the Mix, Melody and Rhythm views, is a card (#212), a summary over details (`Card`); the Song view is the transport's companion rather than a card, read whole at a glance.
  The summary is one row: the title ("Style", "Metrum", "Key", "Band"), an optional glyph (a small living picture, decoration, hidden from a screen reader), the main element in large type and, only for something secondary that changes (the metrum's meter and swing, what the lead is doing), a sub element under it, with the chevron at the top right (`summary.chevron`, in `app.css`), so a stack of them lines up.
  A card never changes size while it plays, so the layout never shifts under a person looking for the next chord: every line of the summary is one line (`nowrap`, cut short with an ellipsis and the full text as its tooltip), the main element's type shrinks with the room there is (`clamp` on container units, so "Cmaj7 · IIImaj7" fits a phone in one line), and the sub element keeps its line when it is empty.
  Where the screen is short (`max-height: 500px`, a phone on its side) the summary gives up its third line: the sub element sits beside the title, the glyph shrinks to `--glyph-compact`, and the page's gap and the cards' padding tighten, so a closed card is about 60 px, not 98.
  On the band's screen a check in `band.e2e.ts` puts the views on a rail at the left at 844 x 390 and 667 x 375.
  So a line says little and shortly ("Intro 3/8", not "Intro · bar 3 of 8"), and a check in `band.e2e.ts` samples the shell's regions and every card's height for some 20 s of playing at 390 x 844 and 360 x 740 and needs each to stay within a pixel (rounding), with the page never scrolling.
  The main element may instead be a control worked from the summary (a snippet: the Sound card's volume fader, the lobby's name field); a click anywhere on the summary, its edge included (the card's padding is the summary's), folds or unfolds the card unless it lands on an active element (a button, a field, a slider, a link, a label).
  A card with no details is its summary alone (a `role="group"`, no chevron): the lobby's.
  `open` is bindable, so a parent can unfold a card: the play card does when a track is chosen.
  The details are free to arrange their content, in `Group`s where they have parts (a level 3 group is a section, a level 4 group a part of one).
  A glyph is drawn on `Glyph`'s frame, a 40-unit square that fills `--glyph-size`, in the colour roles `--glyph-lit`, `--glyph-dim` and `--glyph-accent` (`app.css`), and moves only by transitions, which `app.css` stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`; glyphs live in `components/glyphs/` and take a slice of what the card's details already draw, so the two can't disagree.
  `Reading` is a label and its value on one line in the details.
  A card owns whether it is open: `open` is read once (`untrack`) into the card's own state, bound to the element, and only a person's click changes it.
  Svelte 5 sets an element's attributes from props together, so an attribute the page also changes, such as `<details open>`, is set back whenever another of them changes: a card's headline changing folded it shut (#225, #214).
  The parts under a card are shared too: `Nudge` (two buttons joined in a pill, a step down and up, with what it reaches between them: the tempo's, the key's and the style's axes), `Gauge` (a track with a tick at its centre, a marker, an optional fill from the centre and an optional band of reach: the tempo's licence and the swing), `Segmented` (a row of exclusive segments, the key's scale and the melodic scale) and `Ruler` (a span of steps ticked, with marks laid on it at places that need not be ticks: the scale's notes on the octave's 12 steps, the bar's steps on its 16).
  A new view of one thing builds from these, and a makeover of one changes all.
  A card's details say values, not sentences: "Reaches 117–180", "Swing 62 %", "Pulse 3 of 4".
- The Sound card (`App.svelte`, `sound.ts`, `SoundPanel.svelte`) is how this device sounds.
  Its glyph is an equalizer of four bars (`Equalizer`, `equalizerLevels`): one per performer of the tracks, lifted by the notes it sounds (`player.sounding`) and scaled by the volume, so it is a flat row when stopped or muted; its main element is the volume fader; its sub element says the synth and the MIDI ports chosen (`soundSummary`: "Synth · MIDI out Launchpad, in all inputs").
  The details are two sections: the built-in synth (one switch) and MIDI, which is the connection (Connect MIDI, then Refresh ports and All notes off) with two parts, the output (port, channels, drum notes, clock) and the input (port, and passing what you play on to the output).
  The click's volume in the metrum is "Click volume", so "Volume" is the master's alone.
- The metrum is the page's one section on time (`Metrum.svelte`): the headline is the tempo, the line under it the meter of the bar heard and the swing as the share a beat's first eighth holds ("4/4 · swing 62 %", 50 straight, 67 the triplet, and "· 16ths 67 %" where the sixteenths shuffle), its glyph a beacon (`Blinker`: the bar's eighths as lamps round a circle, the downbeat at the top, the offbeats slid later by the swing, the eighth heard lit, swelling with a ring going out, the downbeat's the widest), and unfolded it holds the tempo nudge and its licence gauge, the swing gauge (the whole range, none at its start), the bar drawn on a `Ruler` (through `Bar.svelte` and `bar.ts`, which draw any meter's bar, not only the song's) and the click.
  The lit mark, on the glyph and on the ruler alike, is the eighth of the step heard (`eighthHeard`), so it follows the band whether or not the click is on.
  The bar's marks sit where the click sounds under the swing (`barMarks`, on the engine's `clicksOf`): a pulse's mark is where its click sounds and never moves, the eighths are counted 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & and sound only where the click is asked to (below), the sixteenths between are faint, so a light swing shows on them and only a swing past half moves the eighths.
  The swing is read there and set in the style, where it stays an axis.
  The song's meter is read, never assumed (#262): the player holds the meter asked for (`Player.meter`, `setMeter`, the Metrum's `Meter for the next section`, which the band takes at the next section as it takes a key) and the song's clock as far as the bars decided reach (`Player.clock`, the engine's `clockThrough`); `hudOf`, `tracksOf`, `playedIn`, the metronome and the Metrum read bars, beats and pulses off that clock, and `BarView.meter` is the bar's own.
  The tracks' grid frames its five bars as long as their meters (`TracksView.frame`, `span`): `Lane` draws a line at each pulse and the bar's start stronger, `BandCard`'s bar numbers take columns as wide as the bars, and `Contour` draws a motif on its own cycle.
  `meter-choice.ts` lists what can be asked for: the nine named meters and the other groupings of 5/8 and 7/8, a mode of a named meter shifted to its downbeat.
  The song's first bar takes the meter asked for, so the count-in is a bar of it; a client's is the meter of its host's first bar once that has arrived, else 4/4, and its own asking rests: its Metrum shows the meter its host's bars are heading for (`Hud.headingMeter`, the last bar decided's, as `heading` is the key's).
  A host keeps the meter asked for across a reload: the snapshot of a session carries it (`HostSnapshot.meter`, saved as it is asked through `Hosting.meter`), and one saved before it asks for 4/4.
- The click has no clock of its own (#166): the player hands the `Metronome` the steps the transport laid on each tick (`Metronome.lay`), it reads them into clicks on the song's meter through the swing (`clicksOf`, `packages/engine/src/metronome.ts`: the pulses, the downbeat first, and the eighths or sixteenths between them only where a `Subdivision` asks, since a click on the 'and' of a bar of quarters reads as a second pulse) and plays them on the player's output (`Output.click`, the built-in synth alone, never MIDI, on the bus `silence` fades).
  So it starts on the band's downbeat, follows a tempo glide, and rests while the band is stopped: switched on, it waits for the next song (decided in #166).
  A client clicks its host's steps on its own output at the skew it plays them; the click is the client's own, so it is never in a session and its switch never rests.
  Only its volume is saved, in `localStorage`; whether it is on never is.
- The players nudge a default the layer above sets; they don't select it.
  The layer above gives the default and a licence, how far a nudge may move it (`packages/engine/src/licence.ts`): the performer's licence on a note (`performer.ts`, T17) is the same shape one level down.
  `licenceOf(style)` gives all four widths from the style: the performer's feel, the tempo's reach, the key's and the melodic scales'.
  The genre sets the range and the players' entropy, which no preset sets, how much of it is used.
  The conductor reads the licence from where the style is going after an intent (`styleAfter`), so a preset or slider that narrows it takes the nudge and the key wish back within it at once.
  The style sets the tempo, and `TempoNudge.svelte` (in the metrum) nudges it by a ratio, 2^(1/24) a step, within `tempoLicence`: two steps either way at entropy 0, rising to a third of an octave at entropy 1, cut by the tempo axis's ends.
  The track holds the tempo played, the style's times the nudge, so the transport, the score and a session's clients read one tempo; the player carries the nudge (`Player.tempoNudge`), a preset's tempo takes it over, and `unnudged` reads the style's own back for naming.
  A key picked is held within `keyReach` fifths of the home key's signature (`intoReach`, `fifthsApart`: relative keys lie 0 apart), and the wheel dims the tonics beyond it.
  The home is the plan's key, the one the band is heading for (`player.home`, `hud.heading`), and before anything is decided the key the song opens in; not the harmonic history's centre, which trails a key change by half a section and may miss it where the harmony is thin (E19, C8). The wheel's radar draws the history, where the band has been.
  The reach is the harmony's, `harmonicReach` (6 × `harm`, rounded), with a floor of one fifth for the players, so Techno gets one and Swing jazz every key; the page reads it from where the track is going (`keyReachOf`), as the conductor does.
  The melodic scales the lead may walk over the key are the harmony's too (`licenceOf(style).melodic`, #209): the plain scale always, the pentatonic from `harm` 1/3 and the blues from 2/3, so Techno, Funk and Ambient offer the plain scale alone; the default is `melodicDefault(style)`, the plain scale everywhere, and a scale picked beyond a narrowed reach is held to the widest it reaches (`melodicWithin`); the page reads it from where the track is going (`melodicReachOf`).
  Whether a nudge held at an edge should move the style itself is open (#176).
  A client doesn't have its host's nudge, so it names a nudged style by the tempo played: a known seam.
- The Melody view (`Melody.svelte`, `Roll.svelte`, `rollView.ts`, #327) is a roll over the keys, its columns the keys themselves, so a note falls onto the key that plays it: the steps ahead above a line where the step heard is (`ROLL_AHEAD` = 24 steps, a bar and a half of 4/4), those played below it (`ROLL_BEHIND` = 12), every place a fraction of the roll (`rollY`, `rollNotes`, `rollBars`).
  The band's lead ahead is outlined (foreseen, from the band card's lanes, `TracksView`), the band's played amber, a player's in their colour (a cell heard from another player, `heardAs`, has no pitches and is left off); a faint column marks each note of the scale and a dashed line each bar's start.
  The lead walks `LEAD_RANGE`, C4 to D6, wider than the keys, so a note above the keys is a thin mark at the roll's right edge and one below them at the left (`beyond`), telling a person which way to move the octave.
  Its header says the chord and the key and holds the scale lock (`scaleLayout`: the melodic scale's notes alone, each as wide, named on the keys, so no note struck is out of it) and the octave, two either way of C4–E5; both are this device's and change nothing the band plays.
  `Keyboard` draws whatever keys it is given (`keys`, default `keyboardLayout`), its height the parent's `--keys-height`.
- `Keyboard.svelte` marks the notes of the melodic scale the lead walks over the key heard as a dot (`hud.bar.melodic`, from `hud.ts`, #209), the safe notes to play along on, while `hud.bar.scale` stays the key's own, which the chords card spells; it and `DrumPads.svelte` flash amber for the band's own lead and drum notes (`player.sounding`, `apps/web/src/sounding.ts`), never what a person plays along with it.
  Any note played, from a pointer, a computer key or MIDI, goes through `App.svelte`'s `onHumanNote`, which sounds it at once (`Player.playAlong`) and logs it for the Muse (`Player.humanNoteOn`, and `humanNoteOff` for its release) alike; `midi/surfaces.ts`'s `partOf` reads a MIDI note's channel to tell the pads from the keyboard, channel 10 the drums' as the prototype's is.
- The timing test (#204, H7) is the door's Timing view (`screens/TimingTest.svelte`).
  It plays a two-sound pattern (a kick and a snare: quarters, eighths or syncopated, at 80, 100 or 120 bpm) for eight bars after a bar of hats, on this device's own synth and never MIDI out, all scheduled ahead on the audio clock (`timing-test/session.svelte.ts`, `TimingTest`, `pattern.ts`).
  The player follows every hit with any input: the computer's keys (a–l, 1–7), the tap button, a pad or a MIDI key of any channel.
  Each tap is timed by its event's own timestamp (`KeyboardEvent.timeStamp`, `PointerEvent.timeStamp`, and the MIDI message's, which `NoteHandler` now carries as its third argument), put on the pattern through the output's pairing with the page's clock (`ClockOffset.fromDeviceMs`), so it falls against what the player heard, output latency included, and matched at once to the nearest hit not answered yet within half the least gap between two hits (`match.ts`).
  The screen shows the bar as it runs, the hits, the taps as dots coloured early, on time (within 25 ms) or late, and in words how far off the last one was; taps sound as a soft click.
  Every finished run is kept whole in `localStorage` under `jambot:timing-runs` (`runs.ts`), a JSON list a browser may refuse, and `run.ts` says what it holds: every hit with its scheduled audio time and the time estimated heard, every tap with the event's time, the page's time and the audio clock when it was handled, where it fell and what it answered, the samples of the clock pairing, and the device (latencies, sample rate, MIDI inputs).
  The runs download together as one JSON file with all of it, or as a CSV with one row per tap, for analysis elsewhere; a run that is stopped is not kept.

- The metronome is the door's Meter view (#259, `screens/Meter.svelte`, `meter/`), for hearing a meter: a bar of any of the nine `METERS`, looped on this device's own synth (never MIDI out), scheduled ahead on the audio clock (`meter-player.svelte.ts`, `MeterPlayer`).
  Its tempo counts quarters, so 6/8 and 3/4 at one tempo are one bar; a change of meter, tempo, swing or layer takes hold at the next bar.
  The click sounds the pulses; `Click between the pulses` adds the eighths or the sixteenths, and the screen says where a swing moves nothing the click sounds (a pulse never moves).
  Its swings are shares, as a sampler's groove percentage reads: `Swing` is the share of a beat its first eighth holds (50 % straight, 67 % the triplet, 75 % the dotted figure, under 50 % the offbeat early), and `Secondary swing` the same for the sixteenths of each half-beat (`leanMap`, `packages/engine/src/swing.ts`, which takes a lean per level; the style's swing axis is one path through the two levels and never leans early).
  `bar-plan.ts` writes a bar's onsets from the meter alone (click, drums with the engine's backbeat (`backbeatOf`), bass), and the hemiola moves the bass onto the other meter's pulses.
  The bass sounds in the band's bass colour at its first style (`timbreOf('bass', INITIAL_STYLE, …)`): a pitched voice reads its saw, triangle, cutoff and resonance from the timbre it is given, and Web Audio refuses the level that is not a number which an empty one makes (`TypeError`), so the note is lost and the bar left half laid.
  `audio/fake-context.ts` refuses it as Chromium does, and `e2e/meter.e2e.ts` hears the bass's saws on the root and the fifth, a quarter apart, beside the click.
  `accentsOf` (`accents.ts` in `@jambot/theory`, since the drums read it too) grades every eighth by a tree over the meter (the bar's pulses halved by `even(2, p)` from four up, the grade the largest group starting there, counted from the root: `*`, `+`, `-`, `.`; 6/8's second pulse is `+`, 12/8's `-`), `Mirror the grouping` reads 5/4 as 2+3, and the accent slider (`meter/grades.ts`) spreads the grades; `ternary.ts` leans a pulse of three eighths, which the band's swing plays straight: late the pulse's eighths last 3, 1.5 and 1.5 steps (a quarter and two eighths, not swung eighths), early 1, 2.5 and 2.5, and its slider is an experiment's (T16, #144).
  The bar is drawn by `Bar.svelte` (`bar.ts`), the same drawing as the metrum's, on any meter.
- The tuner is the door's Tune view (#269, `screens/Tune.svelte`, `tune/`): it listens to a microphone or line in, names the note heard against a reference pitch (A4 = 440 Hz, settable) with its distance in cents and Hz, and draws the spectrum on a log axis with the octaves' Cs marked.
  It is for pitch and never for timing: the input's latency is not measured.
  `mic.ts` opens the input with echo cancelling, noise suppression and gain control off, listens through an `AnalyserNode` that feeds nothing (so nothing is played back), and gives the newest samples when asked.
  `tuner.svelte.ts` looks every 50 ms, through `analysis.ts`: a worker (`analysis.worker.ts`, made at the first look) runs `@jambot/spectrum`'s `analyse`, and a look that is still out when the next is due makes that one skipped, never queued, so the page's clock is never waited for.
  The note is held for six silent looks, and read again from what was last heard when the reference changes.
  The frame the pitch is read over follows the sound: 16 periods of its lowest strong peak, between 4096 and 16384 samples (`estimateAdaptive`, measured in its header), so a low note is read over 171 ms or 341 ms and a high one over 85 ms.
  `plot.ts` holds the spectrum's drawing as numbers (an SVG path, a frequency's x, the octave marks).
  The waterfall under it (`components/Waterfall.svelte`, `waterfall.ts`) keeps the last 100 looks, five seconds, a band a pixel across and the newest look on the top line, in a ramp from near-black through indigo and teal to amber and pale (`shade`), the same in a light theme and a dark one, as a spectrogram is; a canvas, scaled by the page.
  The microphone is let go of on leaving the screen, and a permission the person refused is told in words (`MicDenied`).
  The screen's "Send a note" card holds one note, for tuning an instrument by (`sender.svelte.ts`, `ToneSender`): on this device's synth (played as the band's lead, scheduled for a minute and ended by `silence`) or on a channel of a MIDI output port (a note on and a note off on the channel, and a panic of all-notes-off and all-sound-off), moved by semitones or octaves, followed by a held note.
  It sends its own messages and leaves `MidiOut` to the band, which has its holders and its clock; it is never on under the band, since the tuner is at the door, and a note held is released on leaving its view.
  While a note is held the tuner reads the sound against it (`Tuner.target`), however far off, and not against the nearest note, which is never more than 50 cents away; the needle stops at its ends.
  The MIDI ports come from the Sound card the page shows above every screen, which connects them.
  The synth is an input too (`loopback.ts`, `withSynth`): `Output.tap` hands over the synth's context and the node its sound leaves by, before the speakers, and the tuner listens through an analyser on it as it does through a microphone, never on to the speakers.
  Its context is made only when chosen, so listing the inputs starts no audio.
  This closes a loop the tuner measures: `e2e/tune.e2e.ts` holds A4, then A5 and A6, then down to A1 on the synth, and the tuner reads each within 0.05 cents (the display's resolution) of the exact frequency, and with a reference of 442 Hz reads the same A1 7.85 cents flat, so what it reads is the sound and not the note asked for.
  `e2e/tune.e2e.ts` runs it on Chromium's fake capture device, looping a WAV of a sine 20 cents sharp of A2: the tuner reads A2, +19 to +21 cents, over 171 ms.
- The live correction (#298, slices 1 and 2) pulls a sound toward a scale while it is played, on the Tune screen's "Correct" card (experimental: it adds a delay, the tracker can be fooled, and what it plays is quiet until the person asks to hear it, since a microphone that hears its own output feeds back).
  `packages/spectrum` holds the parts, each tested alone: `PsolaShifter` (PSOLA a block at a time: marks follow the sound's own period to a fraction of a sample, pulses use what was said of their own moment, latency 3.25 periods of the lowest note taken plus a block, about 49 ms at 70 Hz), `PitchTracker` (only the past: a window of 1024 samples, a look every 256), `Corrector` (the nearest note of a scale, a glide with a retune time, a hold and a release when the pitch is lost, a limit of 300 cents) and `LiveCorrector`, the chain.

- The tuner's "Tone" card (slice 7, `timbre.ts` in `packages/spectrum`) says what the note is made of, read over the frame its pitch was (`analyse`'s `timbre`): twelve bars, one for each harmonic's level in dB below the strongest (the floor at −60, a missing harmonic empty), a dot above each for how far sharp or flat of its exact multiple it is (a string's partials stretch), and the note's width in cents (the spread of its peak's power about its centre, less what the window gives a steady sine: 0 for a steady note, about 14 for a pitch that wavers by ±20 cents, 22 for two strings 3 Hz apart at 220 Hz).
  The fingerprint is the twelve levels; "Remember this tone" keeps it (`Tuner.remember`, kept when listening stops) and the card says how far the tone heard is from it in dB: within 3 "the same tone", to 6 "a close tone", beyond "a different tone".
  It is labelled experimental, and what was measured is in `timbre.ts`'s header: on synthetic instruments that keep their harmonic levels it holds within 3 dB across pitch and level and keeps every pair over 6 dB apart, and it does not hold for a sound whose levels follow fixed frequencies.
  The synth's own lead is one (its lowpass filter sits at a fixed frequency): from A4 its fingerprint is 4.5 dB off at A3, 4.2 at A2 and 9.1 at A5, which `e2e/tune.e2e.ts` pins, alongside recordings of a saw (no harmonic missing) and a square wave (six missing) and a steady-note reading.
- The e2e checks tell sounds apart by oscillator type and starting frequency: a kick is a sine starting at 440 Hz (its pitch only glides), a snare's body a triangle at 185 Hz, the bass a saw, the lead a square, a chord note a triangle beside a sine 0.3 % sharp.
  The click is a square at 1800 Hz on the downbeat, a triangle at 1200 on the other pulses and a sine at 800 between (`metronome-click.ts`), each starting at its frequency, which `band.e2e.ts` reads to place them.
  A new voice keeps these apart or updates `band.e2e.ts`.
  A note played along is a square too, but starts at once, off the grid; at energy 0 the lead rests, so a square on the grid is a motif the Muse offered.
